Model Legislation

Bills a legislator can carry.

Not abolition. Consent. We maintain two drafts: a general Mutual Consent Divorce Act that can be adapted to any state code, and a proposed Texas Divorce Code that does the full statutory rewrite for one jurisdiction. Protections for victims of abuse are written into the text itself.

Discussion Draft
A Model Act

The Mutual Consent Divorce Act

To amend the grounds for dissolution of marriage; to require mutual consent for no-fault dissolution; to preserve fault grounds; to protect victims of abuse; and to align related determinations.

§ 1. Short title and findings

Names the act and states the legislature’s findings: that marriage is a civil contract and a public status; that dissolution without fault, default, or mutual assent impairs the obligations of that contract and forecloses any meaningful hearing for the non-consenting spouse; and that two states already operate a mutual-consent regime.

§ 2. No-fault dissolution by mutual consent

Amends the state’s no-fault ground so that dissolution without allegation of fault may be granted only upon a joint petition of both spouses, the respondent’s verified written consent, or the respondent’s default after proper service. Modeled on SDCL § 25-4-17.2 and Miss. Code § 93-5-2.

§ 3. Fault grounds preserved

Expressly preserves every existing fault-based ground, adultery, cruelty, abandonment, and the rest, unchanged. A spouse wronged by real misconduct retains every remedy current law provides.

§ 4. Protection of victims of abuse

Provides that a finding of domestic violence, a protective order, or credible evidence of abuse independently satisfies the grounds for dissolution without the other spouse’s consent, with expedited hearing. No victim is ever required to obtain an abuser’s consent to leave.

§ 5. Aligned determinations

Directs that in dissolutions granted on a no-fault basis over a spouse’s objection under prior law, and in all dissolutions going forward, courts shall consider fault, consent, and the circumstances of the filing in determinations of spousal maintenance; custody determinations remain governed by the best interest of the child, with the circumstances of the dissolution among the factors the court may weigh.

§ 6. Reporting

A companion transparency provision: court clerks report annually the grounds cited in each dissolution, whether it was contested, whether minor children were involved, and which party filed, building the public record every future reform will stand on.

§ 7. Severability and effective date

Standard severability clause; applies to petitions filed on or after the effective date.

State Draft
Texas

Proposed Texas Divorce Code

The full statutory rewrite for one jurisdiction. Replaces Tex. Fam. Code § 6.001 with mutual-consent and fault-based grounds; preserves protections for victims of abuse; aligns alimony and custody determinations.

The proposed Texas Divorce Code adapts the general model to Texas’s existing Family Code structure, works alongside the Texas GOP Platform Item 211, and is drafted to be filed in a single bill or carried in sections across multiple vehicles.

Why this bill moves

It already works

“Adopt the South Dakota model” is a different ask than “end no-fault.” Two states run a consent requirement today; couples who agree still divorce by agreement. The reform is conservative in the truest sense: it restores a hearing. It abolishes nothing.

The objection, answered

Victims are protected, in the text

Every serious objection to reform runs through domestic violence. That is why § 4 is in the bill itself, not a talking point: abuse independently opens the door, consent of an abuser is never required, and hearings are expedited.
