Counter-narrative · Running

“No-fault divorce protects victims of abuse” and what it really does

The most common defense of unilateral no-fault runs through domestic violence. The claim is that any reform traps abuse victims. The response is three parts. First, every fault-based ground, including cruelty and abandonment, remains available under our proposal. Second, the model bill writes abuse protections into the text: a finding, protective order, or credible evidence of abuse independently opens the door with no consent required and an expedited hearing. Third, as a factual matter, unilateral no-fault is used overwhelmingly against stable, non-abusive marriages. The DV framing is a rhetorical shield for a policy whose actual use is far broader than its defenders describe.

Counter-narrative · Running

“Nobody should be forced to stay married”

Divorce requires a positive act by the state to dissolve a marriage. The state is not forcing anyone to stay. Under unilateral no-fault the state is forcing the other spouse, and the children, to accept the dissolution. The question is not whether couples can part, but whether the state will dissolve a public legal status on one private person’s say-so.

Counter-narrative · Running

“Fault-based grounds are ancient and outdated”

Fault grounds for divorce were not an ancient curiosity. They were the dominant regime in American law until 1969 and continue to exist in every state alongside the no-fault option. The “ancient and outdated” frame is a 1960s talking point that bar associations repeated so often it became the water the public swims in. See our history page.
