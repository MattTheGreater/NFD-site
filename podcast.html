The Podcast

The whole argument, one episode at a time.

Not slogans — the actual case. Two tracks of episodes build it from the ground up: what no-fault divorce really is, and what marriage really is in law. Audio feed launching soon; scripts drafted and in production now.

Track A — What the regime is
A·1

The Precondition

Before any argument can land, one reframe has to happen: seeing unilateral no-fault not as “divorce law” but as a unique legal mechanism with no parallel anywhere else in American law.

A·2

Law or Equity?

A divorce decree alters legal status, rights, and title. So is the proceeding law, or equity — and what follows from the fact that it behaves like neither?

A·3

Same Law, Many Masks

“Irretrievable breakdown,” “irreconcilable differences,” “insupportability,” “incompatibility” — fifty phrasings, one mechanism. Why every mask hides the same rule: one will, no cause, no consent.

A·4

Get the Government Out?

The libertarian case examined: if the state “got out of marriage,” what would actually remain — and why unilateral no-fault is not the state leaving, but the state picking a side.

A·5

Privatizing Marriage

No-fault is often described as deregulation. It is better described as privatization of a public status — with the state’s coercive machinery still attached, now answering to one private will.

A·6

Ideological, Not Legal

Whether a marriage is “insupportable” is not a fact a court can find; it is a viewpoint. What it means that the statute compels judges to ratify one spouse’s ideology over the other’s.

Track B — What marriage is
B·1

Is Marriage a Contract?

Formed by offer, acceptance, and consent — and dissolved how? Marshall’s 1819 reservation in Dartmouth, and why the question he set aside is the question of our time.

B·2

Contract vs. Domestic Relations

How the “domestic relations” category grew up around marriage — and whether it can really wall marriage off from the constitutional law that governs every other obligation.

B·3

Status vs. Contract

The synthesis: marriage as a contract that creates a status. Why unilateral no-fault betrays both descriptions at once — dissolving a public status by private will.

B·4

Public vs. Private

Who owns a marriage — the couple, or the community that recognizes it? The deepest fault line under the whole debate, traced carefully.
