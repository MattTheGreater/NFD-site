White Papers

Research you can hand a legislator.

Each paper is short, cited, and written to survive hostile reading — by committee staff, opposing counsel, and skeptical scholars alike.

I

The Dartmouth Exception: Unilateral No-Fault and the Contract Clause

In 1819 Chief Justice Marshall reserved the case of a law allowing “one party to annul a marriage without the consent of the other.” This paper traces that reserved question from Dartmouth through Maynard v. Hill and Richter v. Richter to the modern unilateral statute — and argues the exception has finally found its case.

Forthcoming
II

Status vs. Contract: What Marriage Is in American Law

Marriage is formed as a contract and lived as a public status. Unilateral no-fault treats it as neither: a status dissolvable by one private will, for reasons never stated. This paper names the regime precisely — and shows why neither contract law nor public policy can account for it.

Forthcoming
III

The South Dakota Model: Mutual Consent in Practice

Two states — South Dakota and Mississippi — already condition no-fault divorce on both spouses’ consent. This paper examines how those statutes operate, what actually happens to divorce practice under a consent requirement, and why “adopt the South Dakota model” is the reform a legislator can carry.

Forthcoming
IV

Ministerial Courts: Separation of Powers and the No-Fault Decree

When the outcome is predetermined by one party’s filing, what is left for a judge to judge? This paper develops the structural objection: no-fault converts an inherently judicial act into a legislative one, executed by courts acting in a ministerial capacity — and asks what that means for their jurisdiction.

Forthcoming
V

Personal-Status Pluralism: Beyond Covenant Marriage

Covenant marriage failed not because people reject commitment but because an opt-in checkbox cannot carry it. Drawing on jurisdictions where multiple marriage codes coexist, this paper sketches a genuinely pluralistic family-law architecture — binding codes couples choose, honored by the state.

Forthcoming
