White Papers & Briefs

Research you can hand a legislator.

Each paper is short, cited, and written to survive hostile reading by committee staff, opposing counsel, and skeptical scholars. The core set is below; new entries will be added as we publish.

One-Page Summary on No-Fault Divorce

Twelve Arguments Against No-Fault Divorce

The long-form version. For each of the twelve arguments, the paper develops the point in depth, with supporting evidence and historical context.

The distinction most Americans don’t know they’re missing. Statutory excerpts from Minnesota, Nebraska, New York, Texas, and Virginia showing the singular, unilateral structure of our divorce laws, and recommendations for how to change the language.

VII

The Dartmouth Exception and the Contract Clause

Marshall’s 1819 reservation of the question of a law that lets “one party annul a marriage without the consent of the other” is the structural foundation of our constitutional challenges. The paper traces that reservation through Maynard v. Hill and Richter v. Richter to the modern unilateral statute.
